Brackenridge is forty roofs in a fold of wet land, with a black wood on one side and, on the other, a river that burst its banks in the autumn and has seen no reason to go back. Everything smells of woodsmoke and drowned grass. In the middle, taller than anything has a right to be in a village this size, stands a bell tower with no rope.

As you reach the first house, the bell rings once.

Nobody looks up. That is the first strange thing. The second is the woman taking washing off a line, who stops, puts her hand flat on her own front door, and says to it, quite clearly, 'Wynn Hollis. Wynn Hollis.' Painted on the lintel above her head, in fresh white letters, is the same name. Then she goes back to the pegs, faster.
